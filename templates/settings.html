Where the telescope can point

Two different kinds of restriction live here, and collapsing them into one “minimum altitude per direction” would be the easiest mistake on this page to make. An observatory with a light dome to the north types a high number, and either its northern targets vanish or its telescope is pointed into a wall. So they are kept apart, and each entry says why it exists — which is what tells you, six months from now, whether it is safe to relax.

The arc runs clockwise from the first azimuth. So 248° → 45° is west through north to north-east, the northern half — not the southern one. Reading it the other way round would cut most of a night's rows rather than a tenth of them.
